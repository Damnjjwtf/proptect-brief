1. San Diego MLS signs a direct AI deal through the Global Data Exchange — a second door beside one-off vendor credentialing

Nov 16, company release via MarketersMedia (vendor claim — single release, though it quotes the MLS chief, RESO's chief, and the GDX president) | https://news.marketersmedia.com/zaminai-and-san-diego-mls-partner-to-advance-ai-powered-property-intelligence-through-the-gdx-network/89209777

  • What it is. At the International MLS Forum in Istanbul (Nov 16–17), San Diego MLS (SDMLS) and ZAMINAI, an AI property-intelligence company, signed a memorandum of understanding — a written agreement to work together — through the Global Data Exchange (GDX), the network that lets MLSs and tech companies share listing data across borders using RESO standards (the shared language for listing data). The work: AI built on standardized MLS data that explains a home's value and risk with the evidence shown, aimed at buyers, sellers, agents, and lenders. SDMLS chief Saul Klein is quoted calling ZAMINAI's technology a potential standard for valuation; RESO chief Sam DeBord is quoted backing the model.
  • Why the shape matters more than the product. The release itself draws the line: IDX (the 25-year-old system that lets brokers display each other's listings) was built for display. This deal is an MLS and a vendor agreeing directly to build AI on the data, with the exchange network as the meeting place — not a vendor applying alone and waiting. SDMLS is a founding GDX partner (joined at the 2025 Toronto forum), and the partners say the point is to lay the foundation for similar MLS–vendor agreements.
  • What it means for product opportunity. For Jessica: this is a new, citable precedent for the SFARMLS pitch. The ask no longer has to be "give a small vendor your data under your own one-off process" — it can be "here is the network MLSs are using to work with AI vendors directly, under RESO standards and local governance." GDX is explicitly built to respect local rules and attribution, which is the language a cautious MLS wants to hear. Flag: an MOU is an agreement to collaborate, not a shipped product — no timeline, pricing, or agent-facing tool was announced. Vendor claim until SDMLS or RESO confirms it on their own channels.

2. Bevado raises a $10M seed to build AI agents that remember every resident

Nov 16, Business Wire release, independently reported by Commercial Observer, Pulse2, and Finsmes (funding verified; results are vendor claims) | https://commercialobserver.com/2026/11/ai-platform-bevado-seed-round/ and https://www.finsmes.com/2026/11/bevado-raises-10m-in-seed-funding.html

  • What it is. Bevado (New York, founded March 2025 by former Meta engineer Ruchir Baronia, previously called Frontdesk) raised $10 million in seed funding, led by RET Ventures with Pear VC taking part. Its platform connects a property manager's existing systems into one shared memory — every conversation with a resident or a renter prospect, across voice, text, email, and chat — and its AI agents then run the follow-through on their own: coordinating a repair from report to confirmed completion, calling leasing prospects before tours, chasing stalled interest, and following up on late rent. The company says it already supports managers with portfolios over 30,000 units, tests agents on more than 10,000 simulated calls before launch, and can be live within days.
  • The vendor-claim part. One named customer, Zest Properties, is said to have lifted leasing showings by more than 300% and saved 40+ hours a week on rent follow-ups. Those numbers come from the funding announcement; no independent check exists. No valuation, revenue, or customer count was disclosed.
  • What it means for product opportunity. This is property management, not agent sales tools — but it is the clearest money signal of the month for the pattern this record keeps tracking: the winning AI layer is not a chatbot that answers, it is a memory that acts. RET Ventures brings a network of 50+ investors who own or manage 3 million+ rental units, so distribution is built into the round. The agent-side mirror is still unbuilt in this record: a shared memory across an agent's CRM, texts, email, and showing feedback that runs the follow-through (the Rechat Text item in #28 is the closest piece, and it stops at syncing). Whoever sells the memory layer to solo agents and small teams, rather than to 30,000-unit managers, is in open space.

3. Inform launches: one phone video becomes an auditable 3D record of a home

Nov 17, Business Wire (vendor claim — launch release, read in full) | https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261116383716/en/Inform-Launches-Spatial-AI-Built-to-Change-How-Properties-Are-Inspected-Valued-and-Insured

  • What it is. Inform (founded 2025) launched today with technology that turns an ordinary phone video of a property into a 3D building record with hundreds of structured data points, each one linked to the exact video frame that supports it; anything uncertain goes to a person to confirm. Output is structured to UAD 3.6, the updated appraisal data standard from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Three pieces shipped: Inform Scan, a capture app for iPhone and Android (live today in the US); an SDK (a kit that lets partners build the scanning into their own apps); and early access to Cortex, its property-intelligence engine, for appraisal management companies, lenders, and insurers. Floor plans with square-footage measurements follow the national ANSI Z765-2021 standard. The team comes from self-driving perception (NVIDIA), VR tracking (Meta), and Niantic Spatial, and the founders previously built a floor-plan scanning company.
  • What it means for product opportunity. The evidence trail is the product: every fact about a home traces back to a frame you can replay. That is the same shape the listing side is being forced toward by the Jan 1, 2027 photo-disclosure deadline (an altered photo needs the original beside it) and by the AI-security questions in #32 (show your work, dated). An agent-side version — a listing walkthrough video that becomes a checked, evidence-linked property record an agent can hand a seller or a buyer — is not what Inform is selling (it starts with appraisers and insurers), which is exactly why it is worth watching: if the appraisal side adopts evidence-linked records as the standard, listing content will be expected to meet it next. Reverse-engineer target: the audit design — data point → supporting frame → human confirmation for the uncertain ones — is a reusable pattern for any listing-data product, including Jessica's.

Whitespace

The GDX door. Every access story in this record until now has been a vendor knocking on one MLS's door and waiting — that is the SFARMLS stall in one sentence. The GDX model in finding 1 flips it: the MLS joins a standards-based network once, and vendor collaborations happen inside it, under local governance. No agent-facing product in this record is built to be "GDX-ready" — ready to plug into an MLS the moment it joins the network, with attribution and local rules already handled. That readiness is cheap to build now (RESO's shared package from #32 is the tooling), it gives Jessica a second, lower-friction pitch to SFARMLS ("join the network your peers are joining" instead of "approve us"), and SDMLS — a California MLS — just showed the first AI-vendor signing through it. Watch for whether any Bay Area MLS appears in GDX announcements out of Istanbul this week.
