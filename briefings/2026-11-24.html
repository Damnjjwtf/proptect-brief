1. A sales-only system for property managers — the opposite bet to the all-in-one suite

Nov 24, PR Newswire (vendor wire — vendor claim, read in full) | https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/justa-crm-launches-to-transform-how-residential-property-management-companies-win-new-business-302948434.html

  • What it is. RentScale and CLOZR announced Justa CRM on Nov 24. A CRM is a customer relationship system — the software that tracks leads, follow-ups, and deals. This one is built only for residential property management companies and brokerages that want to win more management agreements (contracts to manage someone else's rental homes). The pitch is narrow on purpose: while other property software keeps adding accounting, leasing, and day-to-day operations, Justa does only the selling part. Four pieces, in the company's own framing: tracking sales (leads, consultations, agreements signed, doors won), sales intelligence (property and rental-market facts brought into the sales conversation), sales experience (personalised outreach, including AI-assisted messages and property-specific video pages the company calls Deal Pages), and sales opportunity (finding prospects, stale listings, and referral chances instead of waiting for enquiries). It is described as an open, AI-enabled system with flexible workflows and ready-to-use playbooks, meant to sit alongside the accounting and operations system a manager already uses, not replace it. Access is early access only, first for RentScale and CLOZR customers, with wider availability planned for 2027. No price is stated in the release.
  • What it means for product opportunity. This is not a listing tool for sales agents — it is the same job one desk over, for managers. The transferable idea is the wedge: pick the one moment everyone else treats as a side feature — winning the client in the first place — and build the whole product around that moment. Jessica's world has the same shape. Agent CRMs bury the offer and disclosure work inside a general follow-up machine; a sales-only, moment-only tool is the pattern that keeps showing up as the opening. Partner angle: Justa needs property and rental-market data inside the sales conversation — that data layer is the part to watch, not the CRM shell. Reverse-engineer angle: the Deal Page (one interactive page per prospect, built from that property's facts) is a small, copyable format — the same page, built for a seller choosing a listing agent, is a listing-presentation product.

2. A mortgage assistant that works the whole deal — and asks before it changes anything

Nov 24, Canadian Mortgage Professional (trade coverage of a launch) | https://www.mpamag.com/ca/news/general/finmos-sidekick-the-ai-assistant-that-knows-your-deals/598297

  • What it is. Lendesk launched Sidekick in open beta on Nov 24 at its Finmo Momentum 2026 event. Finmo is Lendesk's mortgage origination platform — the system Canadian mortgage brokers use to take an application, collect documents, and submit the deal to a lender. Sidekick is an AI assistant inside Finmo that, in the trade report's framing, moves from single tasks to whole deals: it can build and edit deals, manage documents, run down-payment and income checks, calculate the Canadian debt-service ratios (GDS/TDS — the share of income going to housing costs and to all debts), and research lenders, pulling current rates and lender rules through Lendesk's Lender Spotlight. Two design choices are stated plainly in the coverage: by default it asks for approval before changing a deal, and it works inside the broker's existing Finmo permissions — it cannot see or do more than that broker can. The beta is free while it runs. This is Canada, not the US, and mortgage, not home sales — flagged as an adjacent market, not a US agent launch.
  • What it means for product opportunity. The product is not "AI for brokers" — that claim is everywhere. The product is the approval rule. Inman's two-part series this month (Nov 3 and Nov 16, already in this record) found the heaviest AI users in US real estate keeping the tech on a short leash: AI reads and drafts, a licensed person approves anything that commits a client. Sidekick ships that rule as the default setting, inside the permissions the broker already holds. That is the buildable pattern for any MLS-data product Jessica touches: the assistant's value is not that it can act, it is that every action it proposes arrives with the deal context attached and waits for a yes. Build angle: an approval-first assistant layer is a smaller, safer first product than a full agent — and it is the version a compliance-minded MLS or brokerage can say yes to.

Whitespace

The approval log — a receipt for every AI action. Both of today's findings, and the Inman short-leash reporting behind them, point at the same missing piece. Tools now log what the AI said; almost none produce a clean, exportable record of what the AI did, who approved it, when, and on what data. That record is what a broker needs when a client, a lender, or a regulator asks "who decided this?" — and it is what the "licensee in the loop" push (the American Real Estate Association's proposal, reported by Inman Nov 3) would require in practice: a licensed human in the loop is only provable if the loop leaves a receipt. The build needs no MLS licence, because it runs on the brokerage's own systems: for each AI-proposed action (email sent, deal field changed, listing text published, document requested), store the proposal, the source data it used, the approver, and the timestamp, and export it as one packet per transaction. It pairs with the last two editions' paper-trail products — the exposure receipt (#38) and the steering-evidence log (#39): three small tools, one idea, that in a fight about who did what, the side with dated records wins. Honest limit: a log proves an approval happened; it does not prove the approval was careful — the tool should show the context the approver saw, not just the click.
