1. Lundy sells its first enterprise brokerage assistant — the MLS vendor crosses to the broker's side

Nov 3 deployment; WAV Group, Nov 4 | wavgroup.com/2026/11/04/lundy-goes-enterprise-lokation-jefe-and-the-boldtrail-ai-rails/

  • What it is: Lundy — the voice-AI company that built its name inside organized real estate (voice search and a voice add/edit app with MetroList and Rapattoni; Navigator, an assistant trained on MLS rules and policy documents, in MLS and association help desks; nora, its agent assistant, launched by MetroList to 20,000+ subscribers in June 2026) — shipped its first enterprise brokerage deployment on November 3: Jefe, a voice-first assistant for every agent at LoKation Real Estate, a six-state brokerage based in Pompano Beach, Florida. Jefe is LoKation-branded, built and maintained by Lundy, designed with LoKation leadership around the brokerage's own workflows.
  • The rails matter as much as the assistant: Jefe runs inside Inside Real Estate's BoldTrail, LoKation's operating system (agent websites, CRM, lead management, online advertising), alongside MAXA Design for marketing and Sphere, LoKation's own transaction platform, plus the agent's email and calendar. BoldTrail spent 2026 opening itself to AI: Streams (March 31, AI mobile workspace acting on buying signals), Streams Studio (July, no-code, MCP-native workflow builder), BackOffice MCP (August) and BoldTrail MCP (October 13) — front office and back office both exposed to AI assistants.
  • What it means: the vendor that used to sell to MLSs now sells to the brokerage, on top of the brokerage's existing system rather than replacing it. The buyer of agent AI is shifting from the MLS to the brokerage — and the brokerage buys the assistant that already speaks its stack. For Jessica's pitch: an MLS-rooted vendor with a working brokerage deployment is the template for "governed AI access" that doesn't wait on SFARMLS — the same company, the same rules-trained assistant, sold one level down. Flag: WAV Group analysis, not a Lundy press release; the Nov 3 deployment date is the analysis's, and LoKation's agent count is not stated in it.

2. RealtyMark puts 2,000 agents on one AI platform — the mid-size brokerage buys the whole stack at once

Nov 6, EIN News (vendor release) | tpp.einnews.com/pr_news/954905587/realtymark-launches-ai-powered-company-platform-for-over-2-000-agents-every-agent-live-on-day-one

  • What it is: RealtyMark, a Philadelphia-area independent brokerage with 2,000+ agents across eight states, launched a new company platform at realtymark.net, built on Smart Agent Guide. Every agent has been live since October 30. At the front door is AImee, the company's AI teammate, answering the website chat and phone line 24/7 in more than 25 languages and routing each lead to the right agent. The platform gives every agent a page, one login for forms, live MLS listings and search on every page. The full AI tier (Smart Agent Pro) adds a Smart Listing Presentation — branded seller presentation with price range, comparable sales, market snapshot, seller net sheet, and a QR code to the home's own page. Smart Agent Guide and RealtyMark built and fine-tuned it together since the summer, covering compliance and Fair Housing checks, lead routing, agent onboarding, and state-by-state forms.
  • What it means: the pattern from finding 1, from the other direction — a mid-size independent brokerage buying a whole AI platform (website + CRM + AI teammate) as one purchase, not assembling tools agent by agent. The 24/7 multilingual lead-answerer is the feature doing the work: it catches the 10 p.m. inquiry the brokerage used to lose. Flag: vendor release; the "2,000 agents" and "every agent live" figures are the companies' own, and the Oct 30 live date is theirs.

3. Cotality's governed front door for AI — the sanctioned-access reference design gets its award

RETechnology, Nov 5; award Nov 2 | retechnology.com/articles/the-port-is-open-cotality-s-coreai-and-the-new-front-door-to-property-data

  • What it is: Cotality's CoreAI — the intelligence layer threaded through 210+ of its commercial products — plus its MCP Server (launched March 31, 2026), a standard plug that lets any AI assistant connect once, see which data tools its license allows, and pull answers in real time: property identifiers, characteristics, climate risk, ownership data, market trends. CoreAI already powers tools MLS subscribers touch daily (MLS-Touch in the listing workflow, Property Vision, and Aly, a conversational assistant now in beta inside Cotality's Araya platform). In May, Cotality launched Broker Listing Exchange (BLX), a CoreAI-powered, RESO-standards listing-management tool giving brokerages one place to manage listing data and distribution. On November 2, CoreAI won the 2026 Inman Best of Proptech Responsible and Ethical AI Award — the industry handing it an ethics award for building the governed door.
  • What it means: this is the reference design for Jessica's pitch, now with an award on it: governed AI access means the data owner sets the license, the assistant sees only what the license allows, and broker attribution travels with the data. Cotality says it supports 80% of North American real estate transactions and that 1M+ agents use its core technology daily (company figures). The contrast with the gray zone is the point — Solid Earth's 1,200 blocked AI-login attempts a day versus a front door that logs every query. Flag: the Nov 5 piece is analysis, not a launch; the MCP Server itself dates to March 31 and BLX to May. The new bit is the Nov 2 award and the framing — the governed door is now the award-winning shape, not just a vendor pitch.

4. LenDb.ai: a free loan-officer database built for AI assistants — the free-data-as-feed shape, in mortgage

Nov 6, PR Newswire (vendor release) | prnewswire.com/news-releases/draper-goren-launches-lendbai-a-free-database-of-224-000-us-loan-officers-built-for-ai-assistants-and-run-day-to-day-by-an-ai-ceo-302931594.html

  • What it is: Draper Goren Blockchain Studio launched LenDb.ai, a free, lender-neutral database of 224,703 US mortgage loan officers — 48,228 full profiles built from 721 lenders' own loan-officer pages, 512,101 license records across all 51 states and territories — designed for the way people now shop for a mortgage: by asking an AI assistant. Any developer can use its API and MCP server for free; it is listed in Anthropic's Claude Connectors Directory. It is run day to day by "Andrew," an AI CEO answering to a human board. The pitch: finding a loan officer is a biased, pay-to-play game, and LenDb is lender-neutral — not a lender, not a referral service, lenders cannot pay for placement.
  • What it means: mortgage-adjacent, but the shape is the one JJ's open-data thread tracks — a large public/licensed dataset packaged as a free feed for AI assistants, distributed through the assistant's own connector directory rather than through a website. If a loan-officer directory can be an MCP server, so can an agent directory, a comp set, or a listing feed — the distribution is the product. Flag: vendor release; the record counts and the "AI CEO" framing are the company's own, unverified.

Whitespace

The stack-agnostic AI teammate for the independent brokerage. Every deployment this week rides one vendor's stack — Jefe on BoldTrail, AImee on Smart Agent Guide, Aly inside Cotality's Araya. The 2,000-agent independent brokerage that doesn't run BoldTrail has no off-the-shelf AI teammate that plugs into whatever CRM and MLS it already has. That gap — one assistant, any stack, with the brokerage's own rules and Fair Housing checks baked in — is the opening that survives whichever platform wins the brokerage.
