1. Compass sues 15 MLSs in federal court — the listing-rules fight is now a 15-front war

Nov 12, Inman (read in full) + HousingWire + Real Estate News + Bloomberg Tax | https://www.inman.com/2026/11/12/compass-lawsuits-mlss-antitrust-litigation/ | https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-15-mls-lawsuits/ | https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/11/12/compass-escalates-battle-with-mlss-suing-15-in-federal-court

  • What it is. Compass filed 15 separate federal antitrust lawsuits on Nov 12 against 15 MLSs in eight states, accusing them of illegally fining agents — penalties it says can reach $15,000 — for publicly marketing homes outside the MLS. The targets: six in California (bridgeMLS, Bay East, Contra Costa, MLSListings, MLS-CLAW, San Diego MLS), three in North Carolina (Canopy, Hive, Doorify), plus ARMLS, REcolorado, NorthstarMLS, Houston Association of Realtors, Stellar MLS, and OneKey MLS. The suits largely mirror Compass's Oct 16 complaint against CRMLS and seek triple damages plus court orders blocking the rules. Eight of the 15 are already suing Compass in two separate New York federal cases. SFARMLS is not among the 15.
  • The second front inside the same fight. The suits sit on top of Compass's Sept 23 demand that MLSs stop sending its agents' production and listing data to recruiting vendors (Courted, BrokerMetrics, Brokerkit) — the "weaponized data" argument Inman covered Nov 9. Compass says no MLS has agreed; its CRMLS suit claims it lost millions in commissions to agents recruited away using MLS-sold data. So this is two fights at once: whether listings must enter the MLS once publicly marketed, and who controls the data the MLS sells onward.
  • The numbers Compass itself put in its filing. Its complaint says that where MLSs allow it, about 48% of Compass sellers used phased (pre-MLS) marketing, and that those homes sold for 4.6% more. Both figures are Compass's claims in its own pleading, not independent findings — but they are the numbers the other side now has to answer in court.
  • What it means for product opportunity. The rules that decide when a listing must enter the MLS — and therefore what data exists, where, and who can use it — are being litigated in 15 courts at once, with the forum fight (which court hears it) likely decided first. For Jessica: the permission question her product depends on is now case law in motion, and the answer could split by state. A product built to work with listings that live outside the MLS first (private marketing, price testing, showing feedback) is betting on the Compass side; a product built strictly on MLS feeds is betting on the cooperation rules holding. Build for both states of the world — take the listing in once, route it to MLS and non-MLS channels with the right status flags, and keep the audit trail. Watch Nov 20 (subpoena-quash hearing) for the next procedural move.

2. NAR's own tech stage picked the licensee-policy assistant — BrokerBot wins the Pitch Battle

Catch-up: Nov 8, NAR (primary source) | https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/technology/brokerbot-claims-top-prize-in-nars-2026-pitch-battle

  • What it is. At NAR NXT in New Orleans, BrokerBot took the $15,000 grand prize at NAR Tech & Innovation's 2026 Pitch Battle (six startups, eighth annual). BrokerBot builds AI assistants trained on a brokerage's own policies, documents, and processes — agents use it for transaction questions, CRM tasks, marketing, and admin. NAR's writeup positions it as a more secure alternative to public chatbots for sensitive client information. BrokerBot was already in NAR's 2026 REACH accelerator, and co-founder Jerimiah Taylor was named one of NAR's 2026 Innovators of the Year. Crowd Favorite went to KytherID, an identity-verification tool that lets buyers submit offers without initially revealing their names, sharing verified personal information only as needed — pitched as reducing discrimination, fraud, and errors.
  • What it means for product opportunity. This is the licensee-in-the-loop shape from #29 winning NAR's own showcase: the assistant that knows the brokerage's rules and keeps client data inside the brokerage. Any agent tool that touches transactions should now be able to answer "which brokerage policy did you follow?" — BrokerBot just made that the award-winning feature. KytherID is the more direct signal for Jessica: blind offers plus verified identity is a product shape NAR's own audience voted for, and it sits squarely in the offer-outcome space — identity verification first, names revealed only as the deal progresses. Partner or reverse-engineer the pattern, not the product: the mechanic (verify identity, hide names until needed) is portable to any offer tool.

3. RESO puts a fraud flag in the dictionary — and the certification list still has no SFARMLS

RESO Monthly, Nov 9 (read in full) | https://www.reso.org/blog/reso-monthly-11-2026/

  • What it is. The November RESO Monthly adds detail beyond #29's certification-cliff item. The Data Dictionary workgroup approved an EventTypes multi-select field on the EntityEvent resource (v2.3) to signal fraudulent listings and other events, with lookups Delete Fraud, Delete Compliance, and Canceled. Data Dictionary 2.2 is approved for release at the end of Q4 2026; the Queue Resource is deprecated in v2.3 in favor of EntityEvent. Newly certified on DD 2.1: Amelia Island–Nassau County AOR, MIBOR, Momentum MLS. Newly certified on Web API Core 2.1.0: Heart of Missouri Board of REALTORS®, ValleyMLS.com. New DD 2.0 certifications in the past month: Cape Cod & Islands, Hive MLS, Lakeway Area AOR. SFARMLS appears on none of the new-certification lists. Board voting runs Nov 16–30; the International MLS Forum is Nov 16–17 in Istanbul.
  • What it means for product opportunity. Fraud is becoming a structured field, not a rumor: once MLSs can flag a listing as Delete Fraud in the data itself, downstream tools can filter, warn, or block programmatically instead of discovering bad listings after the fact. For an offer product, that field is a free data-quality input — worth building the check for now, before it is widely populated. The certification clock is unchanged and still the lever for Jessica's SFARMLS ask: all endorsements expire Jan 1, 2027 (48 days), and SFARMLS's DD 2.1 status is still unconfirmed from public sources — the question stands, and the ask should include it.

4. PropertyScout buys an AI team to automate the brokerage, not license a tool

Nov 11, Dealroom (single-source) | https://dealroom.co/news/172652-propertyscout-buys-superagent-s-ai-team-to-automate-thai-brokerage/

  • What it is. Bangkok-based PropertyScout acquired the technology and engineering team of proptech startup Superagent (terms undisclosed; founder Yuriy Braterskyy joins as chief product officer). Superagent built software connecting property data, customer conversations, and brokerage workflows to automate repetitive tasks. PropertyScout's stated ambition is an AI-native brokerage platform that automates 80% of agents' routine work — a stated ambition, not a demonstrated result. The firm cites 700+ exclusive agents and 8,000+ partner agents and expansion across Southeast Asia.
  • What it means for product opportunity. The pattern travels even if the market doesn't: a brokerage buying an AI team outright instead of licensing another vendor's tool. When brokerages acquire the automation, the vendor's customer becomes a competitor. For a small builder, the takeaway is to sell the workflow a brokerage can't easily acquihire — the part that needs outside data, cross-brokerage comparison, or a network effect — not the routine-task automation a funded brokerage can now buy as a team.

Whitespace

The pre-MLS data layer. Compass's filing puts numbers on a phase of the deal that currently lives nowhere structured: where MLSs allow it, ~48% of its sellers market before the MLS, and Compass claims those homes sell for 4.6% more (its own claim, unproven). Whatever the courts decide, a growing share of homes is being price-tested, shown, and negotiated before it ever enters the MLS — and the data about that phase (price-test feedback, showing counts, buyer interest) has no home. A tool that captures the pre-MLS phase and hands it to the listing agent as a priced, timestamped record is the unbuilt layer — and it is valuable on either side of the verdict, because the record is useful whether the home then goes to the MLS or stays private. The catch: the 48% and 4.6% figures are Compass's advocacy numbers; verify them against a second brokerage's data before building the pitch on them.
