1. Open Real Estate picks up real buy-in — 826 support letters, first MLS backers named (Nov 2–3)

  • Sources: https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/11/02/new-listings-platform-gaining-buy-in-from-brokerages-mlss · https://www.inman.com/2026/11/03/open-real-estate-who-controls-the-feed-and-who-gets-paid/
  • What it is: Open Real Estate (ORE) — the shared listing pipe launched Oct 26 by AGNT (eXp's parent), HomeServices of America, Keller Williams, and Real REMAX Group — now reports 825–826 letters of support from brokerage leaders, 59% of them from independent firms, plus support from four regional MLSs (the first MLS backers named; the reports do not name all four in the excerpts checked). Early participating brokerages named include Brown Harris Stevens, Lamacchia Realty, Samson Properties, and Windermere. The pitch stays the same: enter a listing once in standard fields, share it with participating brokerages and local MLSs at the same time, free to join, with participating brokerages sharing revenue equally per listing. Inman's Nov 3 analysis names the real question: who controls the feed and who gets paid — if a brokerage-led pipe becomes a major distribution point, local MLSs keep their rules and tools but lose their role as the place listings flow through. The same analysis flags the tension in ORE's own rules: a "participant view" category lets listings sit visible to participating agents without public marketing, which sits awkwardly with the word "open."
  • Product opportunity (watch the vendor pick, not the press release): ORE's own FAQ says technology-vendor selection runs through Q1 2027. Whoever builds that pipe — and whatever standard it sets for listing entry, distribution, and revenue sharing — becomes the template every later entrant copies. For JJ, the opening is not another pipe; it is the layer around the pipe: destination choice, exit terms, and per-listing payment tracking for brokerages that join. That is briefing #16's permission-and-exit whitespace, now with a dated decision window (Q1 2027) attached.
  • Flag: support-letter counts and the "equal revenue sharing" model are the founders' own figures; no MLS acceptance, governance, or vendor pick is final. Still a plan, not a working platform.

2. HouseCanary pushes MLS listings into Google search — three more brokerages, 1,000+ agents (Nov 4, in-window)

  • Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/housecanary-expands-google-listings/
  • What it is: HouseCanary signed three brokerages — RE/MAX Advantage, Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices Florida Properties Group, and ENRG Realty — to its Google Listings Program, putting active listings for more than 1,000 additional agents into Google mobile search in live markets. The program (launched early 2026, slated to expand to all eligible US markets this fall) works by HouseCanary negotiating agreements with MLSs to send member brokerages' listings to Google. Participating MLSs named in the report: Arizona Regional MLS, Bright MLS, California Regional MLS (CRMLS), IRES MLS, Permian Basin Board of Realtors, REcolorado, and San Diego MLS.
  • Product opportunity (partner / reverse-engineer): this is the intermediary lane in action — the company that moves listing data from MLS systems to the consumer surface captures the first inquiry, and the first inquiry is the business. Note what is missing from the MLS list: SFARMLS is not a participant, and neither is any other NorCal Alliance MLS. For Jessica, that gap is the point: the pipes reaching Google today do not yet include her market, which is either an opening (a first-mover pitch for a Bay Area MLS) or a warning (her MLS is not in the room where this distribution is being negotiated).
  • Flag: HousingWire report based on a company announcement (automation-assisted, editor-reviewed). No pricing, opt-in rates, or inquiry-volume figures disclosed.

3. SFAR itself changes systems — GrowthZone member platform launches, tied to SFARMLS records (Nov 4, in-window)

  • Source: https://www.newswire.com/news/san-francisco-association-of-realtors-launches-growthzone-ams-with-gz-learn
  • What it is: The San Francisco Association of REALTORS (SFAR) launched GrowthZone AMS with GZ Learn built in as its new member-management and education platform for its nearly 4,000 members — one place to manage membership, register for events, pay dues, and take courses. For staff, GrowthZone's Real Estate Edition ties member records to NRDS (the national member database) and SFARMLS in one system. SFAR introduced it to members at the SF Summit on Oct 26, with hands-on training on stage.
  • Product opportunity (a window, not an opening): this is the first SFAR systems news in this record in weeks, and it matters for timing, not for access. Nothing here changes vendor credentialing or opens a data door — GrowthZone is member management, not a listing-data API. But an association mid-systems-change is an association whose staff is already in "new vendor" mode, which is a better moment for a governed-access conversation than a quiet one. The practical read for JJ and Jessica: the pitch draft stays held (no outreach without her go-ahead), but the calendar argument for it just got stronger — SFAR is modernizing its stack right now.
  • Flag: vendor release (GrowthZone as source, via Newswire). No data-access, API, or vendor-program detail in the announcement.

4. Australia: commercial comparable sales go pay-per-row over MCP — the data-layer pattern spreads (Nov 4, in-window)

  • Source: https://it.einnews.com/pr_news/954378583/htag-opens-australian-commercial-property-sales-data-to-ai-assistants-through-mcp
  • What it is: HtAG Analytics (Melbourne) launched commercial sold-property search in its Developer Portal: sold industrial, office, retail, and other commercial property, searchable by location, category, tenure, price, date, and size, through a self-serve REST API and through MCP (Model Context Protocol — the open standard that lets an AI assistant call outside data tools). Searches are billed per row at A$0.075 on the Enhanced tier, first 25 rows free, with a no-code Playground in the browser. HtAG's connectors are listed in Anthropic's Claude connector directory. A live search cited in the release returned 14 industrial sales within 5 km of Huntingwood, NSW, since Nov 1, 2025 — only one with a recorded sale price, which is itself the point: commercial sale prices are usually unpublished ("contact agent").
  • Product opportunity (model to borrow): two things travel to the US even though the data does not. First, pay-per-row pricing for comparable sales — a cleaner model than credit bundles for a comp product, because the buyer pays for evidence used, not access promised. Second, the MCP-plus-API pairing from briefing #19's Homesage pattern, now applied to the hardest comps to find (commercial, where prices are hidden). A US version would still need a lawful data source — county records, not MLS feeds — but the pricing and distribution shape is copyable today.
  • Flag: vendor release (EIN Presswire). Australia-only; sold comps only — no commercial leasing, rents, yields, or for-sale listings. No adoption or accuracy figures.

Whitespace / opening worth a closer look

The pipe-picker moment: build the tool that lets a brokerage compare, join, and leave listing pipes. Findings 1, 2, and 4 converge: ORE is choosing a tech vendor by Q1 2027, HouseCanary is already moving MLS listings to Google through negotiated MLS deals, and HtAG shows comp data going pay-per-row over a standard connection. The pipes are multiplying; nobody is selling the brokerage-side control panel — which pipes carry my listings, what each one pays per listing, what happens to my data if I leave. That panel is the permission-and-exit layer from briefing #16, and the ORE vendor window gives it a deadline. It needs no MLS credentialing to prototype (it tracks deals and destinations, not listings), and it becomes more valuable with every new pipe.
